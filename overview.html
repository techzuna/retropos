    What’s real today, and what isn’t

    The fastest way to lose a restaurant is to demo something that doesn’t exist. Everything on the left is built and covered by tests.

    Built and tested Show this

    • PIN sign-in, three enforced roles
    • Live floor plan by zone, real table shapes
    • Order taking with priced extras
    • QR and cash settlement, printable bill
    • Bookings per table, per service, never double-booked
    • Menu, photos and sold-out switch
    • Several branches under one owner
    • Daily and monthly sales in local time
    • Scheduled backups with self-service restore

    Not built Don’t promise this

    • Guests booking online themselves
    • Kitchen screens or printed kitchen tickets
    • Split bills, discounts, service charge, tax lines
    • Country-specific fiscal receipts
    • Card or online payment capture
    • Stock and inventory
    • Customer accounts and loyalty
    • Off-site backup upload
    • Self-service sign-up for many customers

    Six ways in, in order of how soon

    Where the money can come from

    • NowLicence + installationOne-off, per branch. Earns from day one, carries no infrastructure cost, keeps the “no subscription” pitch true.
    • NowAnnual support & updatesThe highest-value change available — recurring income with no new code. Stop paying and you keep the software, you just stop getting updates.
    • NowPer-branch pricingMulti-branch is already built, so a three-branch group pays more than a single café for the same software. Fair both ways.
    • NowHardware & servicesPre-configured boxes at a margin, menu data entry, staff training, custom bill layouts — and local resellers who install for a cut.
    • Needs buildManaged hostingHighest lifetime value, and the model investors ask about. Designed, not built — and it trades away the offline promise, so sell it alongside, not instead.
    • Needs buildPaid add-on modulesThe “not built” list doubles as a price list: online booking, kitchen display, inventory, loyalty. Each an upsell to a customer you already have.

    Raise these before they’re asked

    What could go wrong

    • Self-hosted means support is hard.When it breaks, it breaks on a machine we can’t reach. The support offering has to be designed around that, not bolted on.
    • Updates need a human.Improvements don’t reach customers on their own the way a cloud product’s do.
    • No central visibility.We can’t see how anyone uses it — slower product decisions, and no usage-based pricing unless hosting gets built.
    • Rivals are better funded.They’ll outspend us on marketing. The defence is the two things they structurally can’t offer.
    • Named gaps lose named deals.Kitchen printing and split bills, most often. Both are planned, and the money handling was built so they drop in cleanly.
